Window Tinting

Dyed Window Tint

Affordable Style & Everyday Privacy

Dyed window tint is a cost-effective solution designed to enhance your vehicle’s appearance while adding privacy and reducing glare. This film uses a dyed layer to darken your windows, giving your car a sleek, finished look.

While it improves comfort by cutting down sunlight and glare, dyed tint primarily focuses on aesthetics rather than heat rejection. It’s a great entry-level option for drivers who want a darker look without the higher price point.

Best for:

Drivers looking for an affordable upgrade with improved style and privacy.

Nano-Ceramic Window Tint

Maximum Heat Rejection. Premium Performance.

Nano-ceramic tint is the top-tier choice for drivers who want more than just a darker look. Engineered with advanced ceramic particles, this film blocks a significant amount of heat, harmful UV rays, and infrared light—keeping your vehicle cooler and more comfortable.

Unlike traditional tint, ceramic film won’t fade over time and maintains crystal-clear visibility from the inside, even at darker shades. It also won’t interfere with signals like GPS, radio, or mobile devices.

Best for:

Drivers who want superior heat rejection, long-lasting performance, and a premium driving experience.

Michigan Tint Law

Know what’s legal before you tint

Michigan’s rules are stricter than most states, and enforcement varies. Here’s the short version — and we’ll confirm exactly what’s legal for your vehicle before we install.

Windshield
Non-reflective tint on the top 4 inches only (down to the AS-1 line).
Front side windows
Tint allowed along the top of the glass, with limits on how dark the rest can go. Ask us for the current legal shade.
Rear side & rear window
Any darkness allowed. Dual side mirrors required if the rear window is tinted.
Other rules
No red, amber, or blue tint. Reflectivity limits apply. Medical exemptions are available with paperwork.

This is a general summary, not legal advice. Michigan tint law (MCL 257.709) has been amended in recent years — ask us for the current legal shade for your vehicle.

Common Questions

Tint FAQ

Is window tint legal in Michigan?
Michigan allows a non-reflective tint strip on the top 4 inches of the windshield and front side windows; rear side windows and the rear window can be any darkness. We confirm the current legal shade for your specific vehicle before we install — see the Michigan Tint Law section above.
Will the tint bubble, peel, or turn purple?
Not with genuine XPEL film installed correctly. Bubbling and purpling come from low-grade dyed film and rushed installs. Our work is computer-cut, installed in a clean bay, and carries a manufacturer warranty.
How long does an install take? Can I drive the same day?
Most vehicles are in and out the same day. A two-front install runs about 1.5 hours; a full vehicle is 3–4 hours. Leave the windows up for a few days while the film cures — we’ll walk you through aftercare.
Is ceramic (XR) worth it over standard (CS)?
If heat is your issue — a black interior, a daily commute, kids in the back — yes. XR rejects far more heat and infrared for a noticeably cooler cabin. If you mainly want the look and UV protection, CS is an excellent value.
Do you offer a warranty?
Genuine XPEL film carries a manufacturer warranty against bubbling, peeling, cracking, and color change, plus our workmanship guarantee on the install. Ask us for the exact terms for the film you choose.
Can you tint my home or business windows?
Yes — we install XPEL VISION architectural film for homes, offices, storefronts, and multi-family buildings, including safety and security film. See the Home & Business section above, or request a site assessment.
