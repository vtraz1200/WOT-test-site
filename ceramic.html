Understanding Your Quote

What goes into your ceramic price

Ceramic pricing isn’t one number because no two cars arrive in the same shape. Four things move it.

  • Paint condition & correction

    The biggest variable. A new car may need only a light polish; a daily driver with swirls and etching needs one- or two-stage correction before the coating goes on.

    Light polish → two-stage correction

  • Vehicle size

    A compact car has far less panel area than a lifted truck, a three-row SUV, or a boat hull. More surface means more coating and more time.

    Compact car → truck, SUV or boat

  • Coating tier & duration

    Entry coatings last a few years. Multi-layer professional coatings last far longer and carry a stronger warranty.

    Multi-year → long-term multi-layer

  • Add-on surfaces

    Glass, wheels and calipers, exterior trim, and interior leather or fabric can all be coated alongside the paint.

    Paint only → glass, wheels, trim & interior

Why a cheaper quote usually isn’t the same job

  • Paint corrected first — gloss locked in, not swirls sealed under the coating
  • Applied by hand in controlled lighting, panel by panel
  • Real cure time before it leaves the shop
  • Written warranty with a maintenance plan — not a vague “lasts 5 years”

Common Questions

Ceramic Coating FAQ

How long does a ceramic coating last?
Our Regular coating carries a 4-year warranty and the Premium tier an 8-year warranty, with proper maintenance. Real-world life depends on how the vehicle is washed and stored — we’ll walk you through care.
Is it worth it over a good wax?
Wax lasts weeks; a coating lasts years. It adds real gloss and chemical resistance, and every wash gets faster because dirt and water release instead of sticking. For most owners who keep a vehicle more than a year, it pays off.
Ceramic coating or paint protection film?
Different jobs. PPF is a physical barrier against rock chips; ceramic is a chemical layer for gloss, UV, and easy cleaning. Plenty of owners do PPF on the front end and ceramic over the whole car.
Why do you need to see the car before quoting?
The price is driven by how much paint correction the finish needs. A newer car in good shape needs less; older or swirled paint needs more. The inspection lets us give a firm number and the right warranty tier.
How do I maintain it?
Regular two-bucket hand washes, a ceramic-safe soap, and no automatic brushes. We can also set you up with an annual maintenance service to keep the warranty and the gloss going.
